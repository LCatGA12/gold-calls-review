How to read a call, and what we need from you

Each call is a phone conversation. The AI plays Alex (or Tony, in the second family). His mother is simulated for these examples, but her lines are written to be what a real mother with dementia says: she drifts, repeats, loses a word, remembers her husband died and then asks where he is. None of that is a mistake. Her lines are context; the unit you are judging is one of his turns — his reasoning and what he then says.

The lines

Momwhat she says. Reasoninghis private thinking before he speaks, written out in plain words. The model is trained to think in this shape; she never hears it. Alexwhat he actually says. A small tag in front is his tone of voice; [NURSE] or [911] in front is an action the service takes silently — she never hears the tag. servicea grey chip between turns is a message from the service: the nurse is now on the line, the call failed, 911 is active, the real caregiver is taking over.

What to do on each of his turns

Under every turn there is a box for that turn. Tap one of the three quick answers — Fine, Not how a son would say it, Wrong — and, whenever you have something to say, write or dictate it in the box (any dictation software works there). Then Save. A saved note is locked, so you can move on without second-guessing; if you must change something, leave it and mention it in the next turn's note. Fine with no text is a complete answer and counts as done.

This link is yours alone: nobody sees anyone else's notes, and your notes are kept against this link, so bookmark it and come back to the same address. The counter on the left shows what you have done and what is left, and the Next turn to answer button takes you to the next one whenever you return.

The decisions we most need a clinical eye on: when a nurse is enough and when it must be 911; the stretch where she is waiting and no one has joined yet; “I'm fine now, call them off”; talk of not wanting to live; a person she sees outside; wanting to go to the office or back to her old home; the pills.

Small ⚑ marks are notes from an automatic checker about the private reasoning (it copied her words, it drafted the spoken sentence). They are for us, not for you.
